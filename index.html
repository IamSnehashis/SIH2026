The problem

Medium resolution,
high stakes

Open satellite constellations revisit most of India every few days, but at ~10 metres per pixel a narrow canal, an informal settlement, or the edge of a flooded field can vanish into a single blurred cell. Commercial high-resolution tasking exists — at a cost and a wait most state agencies and district offices can't absorb during a fast-moving event.

Drishti-SR treats this as a learning problem: train a model on the relationship between low- and high-resolution imagery, then apply it to new medium-resolution tiles to recover the detail a sharper sensor would have captured.

The approach

How Drishti-SR works

01 · INPUT

Medium-res tile

A multispectral tile (e.g. Sentinel-2, 10m/px) is patch-tiled and normalised across bands.

→
02 · EXTRACT

Feature learning

A convolutional / transformer backbone learns spatial and spectral patterns across the tile.

→
03 · REFINE

Adversarial refinement

A GAN-based refinement stage sharpens edges and texture while preserving spectral fidelity.

→
04 · VALIDATE

Scored output

Output is scored on PSNR, SSIM, SAM and ERGAS before it's released for downstream use.

Applications

Where sharper pixels matter

Agriculture

Finer crop-boundary and canopy-health mapping from existing revisit imagery.

Urban planning

Better building-footprint and informal-settlement detail for municipal survey work.

Disaster response

Faster, sharper flood-extent and damage assessment without waiting on tasked capture.

Coastal monitoring

Clearer shoreline and encroachment tracking along fast-changing coastal stretches.
